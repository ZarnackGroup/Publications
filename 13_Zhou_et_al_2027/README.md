# Souce code for "m6A acts as a molecular timer linking splicing kinetics to RNA quality control"

## Abstract

N6-methyladenosine (m6A) regulates pre-mRNA splicing, yet exon junction complex deposition after splicing restricts m6A installation near exon–exon junctions, raising the question of when and how m6A influences splicing. Profiling newly synthesized RNA revealed widespread co-transcriptional m6A deposition, including >1,000 intronic sites, with a subset of sites efficiently methylated before splicing. Integrating m6A profiles with splicing kinetics uncovered an unexpected relationship: slowly spliced introns accumulate elevated intronic and splice-proximal m6A. Experimentally delaying splicing further increased m6A deposition, demonstrating that prolonged intron residence provides an extended window for methylation. Conversely, acute METTL3 inhibition accelerated removal of a subset of slowly spliced introns and reduced their retention, revealing a feedback mechanism in which slow splicing promotes m6A deposition, which reinforces intron retention. Thus, m6A acts as a molecular timer that senses prolonged intron residence and marks inefficiently processed transcripts, linking splicing kinetics to RNA quality control.
